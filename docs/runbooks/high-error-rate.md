# High error rate or low business success

**Trigger and scope.** `BusinessSuccessRateLow` fires after 30 seconds of paired synthetic-purchase budget burn: over 14.4× in both 5m and 1h windows with at least five 5m outcomes, or over 6× in both 30m and 6h windows with at least thirty 30m outcomes. The target is 99.9% over a rolling 30 days. HTTP success can still carry a FAILED purchase. Do not use HTTP 5xx alone to estimate affected customers. The alert has an environment label; inspect that environment's transaction list and incident.

**Triage.** Note the alert's `activeAt` and first failed transaction. Compare the Grafana fast (5m/1h) and sustained (30m/6h) burn panels for the environment; inspect the short-window terminal count and failed transaction IDs. A newly started counter may have less than 1h/6h of history. The counters are process-local and may miss a crash between commit and observation; count durable transaction records for definitive impact. Filter Transactions by failed status, environment and time, then inspect error codes, stage evidence and the original trace. In logs, correlate by `transaction_id` and `trace_id`; do not search raw MSISDNs.

**Differentiate.** `SIMULATED_PAYMENT_DECLINED` is a selected business decline; `DB_TIMEOUT` points to the payment query timeout; `BAD_DEPLOYMENT_PAYMENT_FAILURE` is a synthetic release regression. Compare recent deployment reports and simulation runs, plus payment traces and PostgreSQL spans. A time-adjacent deployment is only a candidate. If failures are in package activation, inspect package and outbox evidence instead of assuming payment.

**Mitigate.** In the local exercise, stop the active simulation in Customer Simulator if its decision IDs match failed transactions. For a verified bad deployment, follow [bad deployment](bad-deployment.md). In a real incident, use the approved service recovery procedure for the failing dependency; do not alter balances or retry with a new purchase idempotency key as a workaround. Record actor, time and reason in the incident.

**Recover and close.** Run new synthetic purchases using the same environment and supported payment methods. Check that new durable outcomes succeed, short-window burn falls below threshold with adequate traffic, and no workflow remains pending or outbox event grows old. The 30-day SLO can stay breached after recovery because historical failures remain in its window. Existing failed purchases remain failed; recovery is demonstrated by *new* outcomes. Move the incident through Monitoring to Resolved only after the service and business signals agree. Record affected transaction count and uncertainty in impact, then create actions for the root cause.
